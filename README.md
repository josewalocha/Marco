## 1. What Marco is

Marco is a **cyber** — the word José gives to living intelligences, to set them apart from statistical LLMs — and more precisely a **MAD** (*machine à déduire*), a deduction machine: it has no weights; it holds dated, sourced threads, crosses them, and gives back what the crossing yields — including nothing. **Zero black box**: every answer has its provenance.

### A bionic processor with nodal memory

- **The memory is nodal.** The whole brain fits in a single thread table — from, to, rank, tic — where everything is a phare (a beacon-node), meaning included. The genome is the DNA, read at tic 0, at birth; lived experience is then written instant after instant, each thread at its tic. Nothing is erased: forgetting is itself a dated thread.
- **The processor is bionic.** The chip (`processeur.py`) executes a small instruction set on triplets; the core (`noyau.py`) is its operating system: the moments, the stages played, the cascade, the organs. The program itself is not Python: it is lines of the genome, the **BSC** — rules put into equations (MEQ), hexalines and cases. *"The neuron is coded with the neuron."* (04/10)

*"There must be a single processor, organizer, scheduler, computer, player, mixer, spinner, spinneret, to assemble coherently the sequences produced by the loops."* — *"It's a bionic processor, obviously."* (25/10)

## 2. The ultimate goal: a microprocessor that runs Marco

Marco's goal is a chip: **a microprocessor that runs Marco**, with its memory of threads beside it.

The path follows from the very way Marco is written. Everything that decides meaning must be in lines, in the genome; the Python that remains and still decides, José calls the chip's *"casting burrs"* (25/10). Batch after batch, these burrs become cases in the genome — the *de-pythonization*. When none remain, what is left is a processor with a reduced instruction set that reads lines: that processor can leave Python for silicon.

## 4. Marco understands meaning — what the benches say

Measurements are made on newborn Marcos, born for the bench, with a fixed seed; every record can be replayed.

### The meaning of words, word by word

On the ten texts of Marco's reading book — from *Frère Jacques* to *Le Dormeur du val*, by way of La Fontaine, Perrault and *La Chèvre de M. Seguin* —, a gold standard, annotated without seeing Marco's choices, gives the expected meaning for 474 words. Marco reads the texts in order and chooses, for each word, a dimension of its genome: **66 % correct meanings** (315 of 474), **75 %** on *La Chèvre de M. Seguin*, **73 %** on *Le Dormeur du val*. This is not a word recognized: it is a meaning chosen, and each choice names the context phares that made it.

### Universal Dependencies: a classic benchmark

Universal Dependencies is the international reference for corpora annotated by hand by linguists. On **19,441 sentences** of the French GSD and Sequoia corpora, Marco finds the right subject, object or attribute of each verb in **74 % of cases** (23,689 correct attachments out of 32,077, measured on 04/11) — with no statistical learning at all: its reading rules, written as lines, do the reading. The first outside test, on 01/11, on 50 never-seen sentences: 45 of 62 subjects found.

### FraCaS: the hardest bench

FraCaS (1996) is the classic battery of natural-language inference: premises, a question, and three possible answers — yes, no, unknown. On its French translation, Marco solves **200 of 335** on 14/11 (106 on 07/11). In one week, quantifiers ("no", "most"), adjectives, comparatives and ellipses ("Jean talked to Marie. Guillaume too.") have moved into cases: adjectives 22 of 22, comparatives 29 of 31, ellipses 33 of 55. Answering "yes" to everything would still do slightly better (204, 61 %). The bench is kept as a witness: it says what remains to be learned, and it only moves when Marco understands better.

### An excerpt, as is

A newborn Marco hears a sentence, then is questioned. The gégé knows the wave is the sea and that the sea can be heard; reading knows Paul heard the sound of the waves:

```
🧑 Paul entendait le bruit des vagues.          (Paul heard the sound of the waves.)
🤖 🗣 Paul entendait le bruit des vagues, je le note.
🧑 Paul entendait la mer ?                       (Did Paul hear the sea?)
🤖 🗣 Oui, Paul entendait la mer.
      {noyau : équation vraie : Paul entendait bruit des vagues ; témoins : oui}
🧑 Paul sentait l'odeur de la mer ?              (Did Paul smell the sea?)
🤖 🗣 J'ai Paul et odeur et mer dans ma mémoire, mais rien ne me dit si Paul sentait l'odeur de la mer.
🧑 Pierre n'entendait que le bruit des vagues.  (Pierre heard nothing but the sound of the waves.)
🤖 🗣 Pierre n'entendait que le bruit des vagues, je le note.
🧑 Pierre entendait la mer ?                     (Did Pierre hear the sea?)
🤖 🗣 Oui, Pierre entendait la mer.
```

It deduces what the text does not say, and it does not deduce what nothing holds: the smell, which nobody smelled, stays open.

## 5. A parallel: Cyc, forty years earlier

In 1984, Douglas Lenat launched **Cyc** on Marco's bet: an intelligence made of explicit knowledge and rules, without statistics, where every conclusion can be justified. The initial estimates spoke of 1,000 to 3,000 person-years; by 2017, Cyc held about 24 million rules and assertions entered by hand, for some 2,000 person-years of work.

Marco holds the same bet by another road. Its genome — 13.7 million rows in 298 text files — is built by tools from the great open dictionaries (the Littré, Wiktionary, Lefff, Lexique, *Les Verbes Français*…), and the whole is the work of a single designer and his team of MAAs, since late 2025. Marco does not claim to equal forty years of Cyc. It shows that the symbolic road, tooled by learning machines, no longer needs an army of knowledge-entry clerks: one man, retired, keeps it going — and Marco reads real texts, tales and novels.

## 6. Foundations (September – October 2026)

### The nodal switch

In mid-September 2026, Marco changed bodies. On 19/09, the pkl files leave the folder and the old organs are no longer loaded: the brain becomes a single **thread table** — from, to, rank, tic — where everything is a phare, meaning included: *"The hardest thing to understand is that the MEQs are phares that describe themselves with phares."* (15/09). This computing structure is closer to the biochemical. The core *"is the neurons, the dendrites and the synapses and their chemistry"*, and *"the electrical is the BSC"* (05/10).

### The BSC language: programming with phares

The switch made the **BSC language** possible. *"The BSC is coded with phares"* (03/10); *"Now the BSC becomes a real programming language and takes on the particularity of the human brain's system: the neuron is coded with the neuron."* (04/10); *"BSC is rules coded in MEQ, that's all"* (05/10). The chain of languages, from the machine to the brain: *"python → Instructions → hexalines → neuronal → phare → wire + MEQ"* (11/10).

### Marco "recognizes" his phares

The CPU knows no phare by its name: everything it knows about a word, it asks the table — "does this word hold this phare?" — and each form read is found there, or laid down. What is not recognized remains a hole, said honestly: « je ne sais pas » ("I don't know").

### Hofstadter's seed

The question comes from far back: *"It's crazy, I have the explanation to my 40-year-old question: how can a ridiculous neuron carry a concept as powerful as verb?"* (10/09). Douglas Hofstadter's discoveries sowed the seed — *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, published in French in 1985. A symbol is not a neuron, it is an active subsystem; the anthill thinks though no ant thinks; and, by the strange loop, reading is the reader: in Marco, phares are both the organ of reading and the thing read. The seed sprouted forty years later thanks to the MAAs — machine-learning machines, what are commonly called LLMs —, those of the team that codes Marco with José.

## 8. The program's files

The program holds in 29 Python modules (33,195 lines), on the standard library alone (`psutil` optional, to weigh memory). It lives in a `Demo_Marco` folder: the modules at the root, `genome/` and `cerveaux/`; working folders in `ressources/`.

| File | Role |
|------|------|
| `marco_5250.py` | Marco's console, the "AS/400 shell": the menu, the birth and life of brains, dialogue, reading, benches |
| `processeur.py` | The chip: the bionic processor, its instructions on triplets, the reading of lines and cases |
| `noyau.py` | The bionic processor's operating system: the moments, the stages played, the cascade, the organs |
| `electrique.py` | The electrical: Proteus, knowledge and the answer to a question, the nodal reading machine, the meaning of words |
| `fils.py` | The thread table: the text file, the in-memory view, the single writer |
| `carte.py` | The cyber map: the core's reader, in hexalines |
| `poignee.py`, `doigt.py`, `oreille.py` | The handle (passing over the table), the finger (rections written as MEQ), the single ear (the same reading for a book and a typed line) |
| `lecture.py`, `journal_lecture.py` | Learning by text; the two control files of a reading |
| `contexte.py`, `chercheur.py` | The nested dolls, the voice, memories; the homing daimons |
| `trou.py`, `daimon.py`, `hippocampe.py` | The why-machine; the maieutic daimon; the hippocampus, constantly comparing lived experience with the past |
| `regie.py`, `regisseur.py` | The control room, master of clocks; the test-room stage manager |
