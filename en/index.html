“AI is neither intelligent nor artificial. So what is it?”

A learning machine.

Marco, on the other hand, is a deduction machine.

No black box: Marco holds dated, sourced threads, crosses them, and gives back what the crossing yields. Every answer says where it comes from; when nothing holds an answer, Marco says: I don't know.

“Neither intelligent nor artificial”: after Kate Crawford, Atlas of AI, 2021.

“It's a bionic processor, obviously.” José Walocha, 25 Oct 2026
1

General description

Marco is a cyber, the word José Walocha gives to living intelligences to set them apart from statistical language models. It is a MAD (machine à déduire), a deduction machine: no neural network, no learned parameters. Zero black box.

By design, Marco is a bionic processor with nodal memory. The processor runs a small instruction set over the lines of the genome. The memory fits in a single table of threads, where everything is a phare (a beacon-node), meaning included. The genome is read at tic 0, at birth; lived experience is then written instant after instant, each thread at its tic. Nothing is erased: forgetting is itself a dated thread.

Marco reads and speaks French. It reads real texts, nursery rhymes, fables and tales: Frère Jacques, La Fontaine, Perrault, La Chèvre de M. Seguin, Le Dormeur du val. It speaks French because its designer does; the system also runs in Mandarin, see chapter 8.

Features

  • MemoryNodal: a table of threads, from · to · rank · tic. Everything is a phare.
  • Genome298 text files, 13.7 million rows, drawn from the great open dictionaries.
  • ProgramThe BSC: rules put into equations (MEQ), hexalines, cases. “The neuron is coded with the neuron.”
  • ProcessorA reduced instruction set over triplets; a core that schedules the moments, the stages, the organs.
  • ProvenanceEvery answer names the threads that hold it. What is not held stays a hole, honestly stated.
  • SleepThe cyber sleeps, reviews its open loops and forgets through a dated thread.
  • Code29 Python modules, 33,195 lines, the standard library only.

FraCaS is the hardest bench. Problems solved out of 335, each time on a newborn Marco. From 14 to 15 November, the temporal section goes from 21 to 66 of 71, ellipses from 33 to 50 of 55. Of the 17 problems missed, the designer holds three to be right; three belong to pairs where the same sentences and the same question expect two opposite answers; four bear on one long sentence, left for the end.

Toward silicon

Marco's goal is a chip: a microprocessor that runs Marco, with its memory of threads beside it. The path follows from the very way Marco is written.

  1. Today

    A processor that reads lines

    The program is in the genome, as BSC lines. The Python processor reads them with a small instruction set.

  2. Batch after batch

    Removing the casting burrs

    The Python that still decides meaning becomes cases in the genome: the de-pythonization.

  3. The goal

    A microprocessor

    When no burrs remain, what is left is a processor with a reduced instruction set, which can leave Python for silicon.

Open work

  • 100% understood: “We aim for 100% decoded and 100% understood.” The meaning gold standard is its measure.
  • Nested dolls: ideas nested inside one another like Russian dolls. “Marie's old black cat” is a single thing, placed inside the sentence; Marco still reads flat, subject, verb, object. Work opened on 16 Nov.
  • Figurative meaning: meaning deduced from the concrete is reached; next comes meaning deduced from a word turned aside, up to the sentence that speaks of itself: « Ce n'est pas un point qui termine cette phrase. » (“It is not a full stop that ends this sentence.”), José Walocha, in the spirit of Hofstadter and Magritte.
  • The gégé: what soothes beside what threatens, the senses born of the body, hearing, smell.
  • Understanding a book: reading Eugénie Grandet, the summary, the characters and the names they go by.

Expert system + Cyc + Lisp + BSC

Marco does not invent deduction. Its designer sums it up in a formula: “expert system + Cyc + Lisp + BSC” (19 Nov 2026). The first three are its parents, all more than forty years old; the fourth is its own.

LineageWhat it bringsIn Marco
Expert system
OPS5, Charles Forgy, Carnegie Mellon, late 1970s; MYCIN, Stanford, 1970s		The engine: rules played from facts toward conclusions, forward chaining. And the why: MYCIN showed the physician the rules that had led to its conclusion.The player replays a stage of cases until nothing new is written. Every answer names the threads that hold it.
Cyc
Douglas Lenat, 1984		Common sense written as assertions, sorted into microtheories, that is, contexts.The lessons: “a porch is a shelter”, “rain fries circuits”. Two contexts where the same rain does not threaten the same thing.
Lisp
John McCarthy, 1958		The program is data: code is written with the lists it handles.A case is a line of the genome, just like a fact.
BSC
Marco		The data is the language.The rule, the fact and the lesson are words of the genome, in the language it reads: no translation between what it reads and what it plays. “The neuron is coded with the neuron”: what its designer calls neural programming.

Marco speaks French because its designer speaks French. The system itself works with any language, ideograms included: a word is a place in a line, whatever its script. The trial is in chapter 8.

What none of the three had

  • It reads. Its knowledge comes from open dictionaries and from what it reads, not from data entry. Writing knowledge by hand is the wall where expert systems stopped, and where Cyc spent forty years.
  • It cares about something. An expert system deduces whatever it is asked; Marco first deduces what threatens what it cares about. That is the awakening.

Marco does not claim to equal forty years of Cyc. It shows that the symbolic road, tooled by learning machines, no longer needs an army of data entry.

Code example: the robot in the rain

On 19 Nov 2026, Marco plays a robot. Its sensors feel rain; one lesson says rain fries circuits, another that a robot looks after its circuits. It looks for shelter, asks the web when the rain will end, counts the time lost and how late it will be. Thirty-one cases in three stages; here are the three that move the clock forward, as they are in the genome (in French).

BSC · robot patch · 19 Nov 2026
TA1 — arrival: start from the departure with the length of the trip
si ( la_journée , départ , un_t )
si ( la_journée , trajet , _|_ , un_d )
alors ( la_journée , avance , un_t , un_d )
TA2 — a step: a quarter of an hour more on the clock, a quarter less to go
si ( la_journée , avance , un_t , un_d )
si ( un_t , puis , un_t2 )
si ( un_d2 , puis , un_d )
alors ( la_journée , avance , un_t2 , un_d2 )
TA3 — arrived: nothing left to go
si ( la_journée , avance , un_t , 0q )
alors ( la_journée , arrivée , un_t )
{départ 8h30, trajet 2q : ( avance , 8h30 , 2q ) → ( avance , 8h45 , 1q ) → ( avance , 9h00 , 0q ) → ( arrivée , 9h00 )}

No case says “repeat”: the player replays the stage as long as it writes something new, and stops when nothing moves. Moving forward is one step along a chain of the genome, ( 8h15 , puis , 8h30 ) (“then”); adding is walking two chains at once; comparing is walking from one toward the other until they meet. No number is computed.

A case, a fact and a lesson have the same form in the genome: one word per place.

The cases still speak a conventional French (la_journée, un_t, _|_); the lessons, free French. The goal: that Marco writes a case itself from a sentence it has read. The robot bench, 20 expected results out of 20, shows the code runs; written alongside the rules, it does not measure understanding. Sources: OPS5, MYCIN, Lisp and homoiconicity, Cyc (Wikipedia).

It feels a drop on its hand, deduces that it is raining, that the rain threatens its circuits, that it cares about its circuits, that the porch is a shelter. It goes there, asks the web, waits, asks again, and adds up the time lost: two quarters, then two more, four (四刻). The sentences in 「 」 are rendered by the bench, as in French: the mouth does not say them yet.

What stays French: the words of the chip

In the first try of the second step, la_journée had become 今天 (“today”): nothing moved, 2 expected results out of 20. The chip recognizes the name it is given, and the unknowns, by the French article that opens them: la_ for the one it is given, the day; un_ for any one, un_t, un_d. Mandarin has no articles. With the article put back, la_今天, everything moved: 20 out of 20. English has articles, but not the chip's: with the_day, nothing moves either, 2 out of 20; with la_day, 20 out of 20.

So the robot in Mandarin keeps nine French words and two articles, those of the chip's instruction set: tient (holds), membre_de (member of), même (same), vrai (true), faux (false), faire (do), ou (or), ensemble (together), joue (plays), and un_, la_. They are the mnemonics of a processor: a chip speaking Mandarin would say 某 (“a certain”) for un_ and 这 (“this”) for la_. That is a table to add to the chip, not a rule to rewrite.

In English, “rain” is both the verb and the noun: the same word holds “verb” and “common noun”, and the robot does not get lost. Some English words also exist in French with another meaning: “water”, “tend”, “drop”. The French genome gives them its own marks; they do no harm, because the robot reads only its lessons.

What the trial does not show

  • Marco does not read Mandarin yet: the Chinese words were given by hand the marks the cases ask for (verb, common noun). A Chinese genome would come from an open dictionary such as CC-CEDICT.
  • The reading cases are written for French grammar. Chinese has neither conjugation nor articles; it has its measure words and particles (了, 过, 着).
  • Chinese is written without spaces: to read a sentence, it must first be cut into words.

The human brain does the same

Chinese and French readers read with the same network, and one region of the left fusiform gyrus is critical for recognizing words in every script; the script shapes the variations. So does Marco: one mechanism, whatever the script; the variations are in the chip's words and in the grammar of the cases.
