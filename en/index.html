Marco, the deduction machine

An intelligence with no black box. Marco holds dated, sourced threads, crosses them, and gives back what the crossing yields. Every answer says where it comes from; when nothing holds an answer, Marco says: I don't know.

“It's a bionic processor, obviously.” José Walocha, 25 Oct 2026
1

General description

Marco is a cyber, the word José Walocha gives to living intelligences to set them apart from statistical language models. It is a MAD (machine à déduire), a deduction machine: no neural network, no learned parameters. Zero black box.

By design, Marco is a bionic processor with nodal memory. The processor runs a small instruction set over the lines of the genome. The memory fits in a single table of threads, where everything is a phare (a beacon-node), meaning included. The genome is read at tic 0, at birth; lived experience is then written instant after instant, each thread at its tic. Nothing is erased: forgetting is itself a dated thread.

Marco reads and speaks French. It reads real texts, nursery rhymes, fables and tales: Frère Jacques, La Fontaine, Perrault, La Chèvre de M. Seguin, Le Dormeur du val.

2

Features

  • MemoryNodal: a table of threads, from · to · rank · tic. Everything is a phare.
  • Genome298 text files, 13.7 million rows, drawn from the great open dictionaries.
  • ProgramThe BSC: rules put into equations (MEQ), hexalines, cases. “The neuron is coded with the neuron.”
  • ProcessorA reduced instruction set over triplets; a core that schedules the moments, the stages, the organs.
  • ProvenanceEvery answer names the threads that hold it. What is not held stays a hole, honestly stated.
  • SleepThe cyber sleeps, reviews its open loops and forgets through a dated thread.
  • Code29 Python modules, 33,195 lines, the standard library only.

It deduces what the text does not say. It does not deduce what nothing holds: the smell, which no one smelled, stays open.

FraCaS is the hardest bench, and Marco is only starting on it: quantifiers (“none”, “most”) are not yet read as such. It is kept as a witness of what remains to learn. Universal Dependencies is read without any statistical learning: the reading rules, written as lines, do the reading.

Toward silicon

Marco's goal is a chip: a microprocessor that runs Marco, with its memory of threads beside it. The path follows from the very way Marco is written.

  1. Today

    A processor that reads lines

    The program is in the genome, as BSC lines. The Python processor reads them with a small instruction set.

  2. Batch after batch

    Removing the casting burrs

    The Python that still decides meaning becomes cases in the genome: the de-pythonization.

  3. The goal

    A microprocessor

    When no burrs remain, what is left is a processor with a reduced instruction set, which can leave Python for silicon.

Open work

  • 100% understood: “We aim for 100% decoded and 100% understood.” The meaning gold standard is its measure.
  • Quantifiers: “none”, “most”, “many”, what FraCaS asks for.
  • The gégé: what soothes beside what threatens, the senses born of the body, hearing, smell.
  • Understanding a book: reading Eugénie Grandet, the summary, the characters and the names they go by.

A parallel: Cyc

In 1984, Douglas Lenat launched Cyc on Marco's wager: an intelligence made of explicit knowledge and rules, without statistics, where every conclusion can be justified. Marco makes the same wager by another road.

CycMarco
Start1984late 2025
Knowledgeabout 24 million rules and assertions in 2017, largely entered by hand13.7 million rows, built by tools from open dictionaries
Effortsome 2,000 person-years by 2017one designer and his team of machine-learning assistants
Reading—tales, fables, annotated corpora

Marco does not claim to equal forty years of Cyc. It shows that the symbolic road, tooled by learning machines, no longer needs an army of data entry. Cyc figures: Wikipedia.
